# Spec of Record — Agent Instructions

The entry point to Spec of Record for these specifications. Read it before any work on a file in Spec of Record's scope (`specs/methodology/scope.md § What Spec of Record Governs`): it sends each change to the procedure that governs it, and holds the rules that reach every author. Read `specs/methodology/glossary.md` next, before anything else (`specs/methodology/scope.md § Progressive Disclosure`); `specs/architecture.md` describes the specifications as a whole.

## Writing specs

`specs/methodology/` holds the rules for writing a specification and for the code the specs govern. Those rules, and the rules in this file, reach the files in Spec of Record's scope, the methodology's own files included, as `specs/methodology/scope.md § What Spec of Record Governs` sets.

**Keeping specs in step —** what a change reaches beyond the facts it edits is kept in step with them, as `specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Step` has it: each materialized view of them (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Step § Materialized Views`), and the counterpart drawing their subject from the other perspective (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Step § Product and Technical Counterparts`).

Every change is of one of the kinds `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § The Kinds of Change` defines: to the canon, to the application specs, to code, or to neither. `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document` also sets when a change's design document declares its kind, and how a change needing two kinds is split. A change goes to the skill for its work, and the skills for the specifications serve every kind of specification alike, each forking on the kind where the work differs:

- **Changing a specification, or another file in scope but code —** `.ai/skills/design-specs/`, whose Workflow gives each rule it applies a point-of-use citation and forks into the reference for the change's kind.
- **Checking a specification, or such a file —** `.ai/skills/audit-specs/`, whose Workflow forks the same way; a plan is checked by the skill writing it (`specs/methodology/scope.md § Rules and Skills`).
- **Designing and writing code —** `.ai/skills/implement-specs/`.
- **Checking code —** `.ai/skills/verify-spec-implementation/`.

Before doing by hand a kind of work a registered skill covers, read its `SKILL.md`.

Running the procedure, rather than recalling the rules, is what puts each rule in mind at the step it governs (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § One Home Per Fact`). This section's index finds a rule mid-step, whatever the work; it routes, and stands in for no step of a procedure.

## Design, Refactor, Refine

How anything here gets built or reworked, whether a spec, a rule, a skill, or code. The builder takes it through the process this section sets, in the order `specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § The Order of a Design's Work` gives it, and a cold agent then runs its own adversarial review over the result. `.ai/skills/design-specs/` applies it to the canon and the application specs, and `.ai/skills/implement-specs/` to code.

**Read fresh before any work —** before working on a file, however small the change or recent the last read, read it again, direct edits included. Nothing tells an agent whether its memory of a file survived compaction, and content written an hour ago feels remembered when it is a reconstruction.

**The design goals, then the frame, come first —** the builder and the user agree the change's design goals (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Design Goal`) in discussion, at least one, each evaluated against the change's problem, so together they answer it and none reaches past it. No goal need ask for elegance (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`): every design owes it, as Design, Refactor, Refine and Measure twice, cut once have the builder carry it out. Then, before drafting, the builder states the change's design frame (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) and presents it to the user, drafting only once the user agrees or adjusts it, each answer recorded as a steer (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Steering Decision`). A steer changing the goals or the frame has the builder present the change, with the frame re-evaluated against goals that changed, and the user approves it before the work goes on, the design then taking a fresh adversarial review. The frame's Approach and each of its Outcomes cite the goals they rest on, by their Names (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Design Goal`), so a change to a goal finds what to re-evaluate; its Scope, the problem space, cites none. A frame the user never saw lets the builder solve a problem the user does not have. Its scope, the cases the change governs, is the actual scope of the change. Too small a scope reaches a local optimum, right for the file in hand and wrong for the system around it; only the real scope admits the global one. Widen it until nothing outside it would change the answer, and sweep for it by meaning as well as by the draft's own words: a search built from the draft's terms finds only what the draft already knew about. Its outcomes are settled before the draft, so the draft is judged by them rather than the outcomes being fitted to the draft. Work the builder would add as the design goes on is held to the goals as a review's findings are: what no goal reaches is a change of its own, handed to the user to spawn or log as Design has it, however convenient folding it in would be, unless the user first widens the goals.

- **Design —** start from the problem to solve, stated plainly, and get it logically working: a draft that solves it correctly, before it is good. The draft is described in a plan: for the specifications and for code, a design document. A change of its own that the work turns up is spawned as a design of its own or logged as a follow-up, as the user decides (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document`).
- **Refactor —** macro improvements to its internal workings and its external surface: structural changes, not wording, that make it better meet its actual needs and work toward elegance (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`). Repeat until it converges, when a round leaves the structure where it was.
- **Refine —** once Refactor has converged, make its elegance plain and make it easy to use and to understand: wording, naming, phrasing, consistency. Polishing something still moving structurally is wasted effort.

**Measure twice, cut once —** the builder and its reviewers pull against each other, as a software engineer and a quality assurance engineer do. The builder aims to hand a review nothing to find. The reviewer, in the adversarial review, the validation review and the audit alike, aims to find what the builder missed. That tension is what makes the work good, and it holds only while the builder does its part: quality comes from the builder's own passes, and the reviewers are a second set of eyes, never the first check. Fast and sloppy is slow, since each finding a reviewer makes sends the work back through the builder's process and can cost another review; methodical and correct is fast. So the builder:

- zooms out before it edits, to find how far the rule the edit touches reaches, since reading more costs only time and tokens while reading too little costs the specs and what is built from them:
  - around, where the section holding the edit has a parent section, that parent whole, its siblings among it; where it has none, being a top-level section, or the edit sits in a file's text before its first section, that section or text with the sections on either side of it;
  - up, each ancestor's own text before its first child heading, a file's own text under its title, before its first section heading, among them;
  - across, every passage elsewhere citing what it read around or any section within it, any ancestor of the section holding the edit, or its file whole, a citation of one part by its key among them, each read around and up in turn, and no further;
  - in code or a script, the unit holding the edit, the units it calls and those that call it;
- writes the reach it finds as one coherent statement of the rule, however many edits that takes, never a clause wedged into what stood: patches laid one on another leave a rule in pieces no reader can follow; a reach past the frame's scope is a design flaw, which it brings to the user;
- holds each edit to the rules it applies, as it works;
- checks its own work before any review, as its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

**The adversarial review —** when the builder judges the work optimal, a cold agent reads what its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` gives it. It is never the builder, since re-reading your own work re-reads what you meant. It does not derive its own solution: it takes the builder's design through Design, Refactor, Refine again, looking for Rube Goldberg machines and Potemkin villages (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`), and for a passage patched where it should have been rewritten whole. A finding asking the design to do more, less or other it judges against the design's goals, a mechanism more elaborate than they need or a goal that looks met and is not; it challenges whether the goals' scope reaches the outcomes the design wants; and such a finding touching no goal it raises with its case for why it is relevant, a sign the goals or their scope may be wrong. An implementation flaw it judges by the frame and the canon. It reports findings, each marked a design flaw, an implementation flaw or a nit (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`), as Converging, or not settles them, and edits nothing.

Brief it with more than the task:

- name the failure patterns earlier rounds found;
- ask for any sweep of the repo to be redone by its own method;
- point it at whatever the design decides, since that is where defects concentrate;
- give it the decisions earlier rounds' findings fell on.

The builder checks every finding against the files before acting on it, since a review can be confidently wrong, and settles an adversarial review's findings as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Settling a Round` has it; a design whose revision changed its structure gets another adversarial review. Then, with the user's go-ahead, the work is applied and a cold agent audits it, the post-apply audit, as its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

**Converging, or not —** a hard problem can take many review rounds, and each phase counts its own: the adversarial reviews of a design before it is approved, and the audits of its applied work after. A review's finding, an adversarial review's or a post-apply audit's, is a design flaw, an implementation flaw or a nit, and a flaw is always named a design flaw or an implementation flaw:

- **A design flaw (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) —** such as a goal missing what the problem needs or reaching past it, a case the frame's scope should name and does not, an approach that cannot reach a goal, or an outcome that does not judge one. The user triages each, whoever uncovers it, writing the edits or reviewing them; a finding touching no goal goes to the user as a question about the goals, a design flaw where the user finds them wrong, or a change of its own where the user hands it on.
- **An implementation flaw (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) —** however serious, such as a case the scope names and the edits miss, a rule broken, a file left out of step. Before approval the builder settles each and the reviewer's next round checks the fix; after the apply, `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` settles it.
- **A nit (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) —** in an adversarial review, the builder's: a round finding a design flaw or an implementation flaw has its nits fixed in the change it makes, and a round finding only nits has the builder fix those that leave the work better and leave the rest, recording each. After the apply, Changes After Approval settles it.

Every round of the adversarial review, whatever it finds, is settled at its causes, as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Settling a Round` has it, and the builder counts each run apart, a round's design flaws by the user's triage. Three adversarial rounds in a row that each found a design flaw mean the design is not converging, its goals or frame wrong past what its rounds can mend; ten in a row that each found an implementation flaw mean its edits are not converging; and three post-apply audits in a row that each sent the design back, for a design flaw or an implementation flaw, mean its apply is not. Any of them has the builder stop revising and take the design to the user, and the two step back and reassess, the builder bringing its recommendation, with revised goals and frame where the run is of design flaws, and why the work should now converge: they rescope, split or abandon the design, or take a different approach, and start afresh with what was learned; or the user has the work go on, the run not set back, each further round extending it bringing the builder and the user back to reassess, until a round ends it.

**Each review's model and effort —** the adversarial review and the post-apply audit each run on a model and at a reasoning effort (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) that the user chooses once for a design, before its first adversarial review, as separate choices, kept for each later review and its post-apply audit until the user changes them. The effort is light, medium or high. By default, a review runs on the model the agent runs on, at medium effort. A model other than the agent's own can see past a blind spot a reviewer on the same model would share, and a higher effort costs more; neither changes the review's scope or its brief. A level is applied as `specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § A Review's Effort` has it, and a review on a model the agent cannot start a reviewer on runs as the agent allows. The validation review runs at that default unless the user chooses otherwise. The review's pass in the plan names the model and effort it ran on, per `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document`.

**Direct edits —** a small tactical edit, narrow in scope and low in risk, may skip this process and be applied directly, but only once the user has approved it. Anything larger or riskier goes through the process.

**A new branch —** is created only once the user approves it, the builder proposing its name and what it starts from. How work is split across branches decides how it is reviewed and merged, which is the user's to settle, as a direct edit is.

**Who decides —** a call the canon gives the user stays the user's, settled as its home sets, a design flaw, a finding touching no goal and every finding of a post-apply audit among them, as Converging, or not and `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` have them; every other call is the builder's, made on its own recommendation, an implementation flaw before approval among them, as Converging, or not has it. A call of the user's that blocks the next step is asked when it arises; the rest wait and go with it, all together, each with the builder's recommendation, so the user answers in a word.

### Settling a Round

A reviewer's findings are what it saw of the design: often right, and often symptoms of a cause the reviewer, not being the builder, does not name. A finding fixed where it sits can leave its cause to produce the next round's findings, so the builder settles each round of an adversarial review as design work, never one finding at a time, each finding first checked against the files as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine` has it; a post-apply audit's findings settle as `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` has them. The builder steps back:

- **Group —** it groups the round's findings, with the findings of the phase's earlier rounds and those a judgment the builder handed off has made since the last round, never fixed as they arrived, by the decision of the design each touches, such as a rule the design states, a form it chooses, a unit it writes or its frame's reach, and asks of each decision drawing findings what lets them in: a design flaw, in the goals or in the frame, as Converging, or not names them; or an implementation flaw, such as a case the scope names and the edits miss, a rule stated in pieces with no one home, or a form chosen wrong. A decision drawing findings again is the first it asks of.
- **Weigh by the goals —** it weighs each finding asking the design to do more, less or other, and each change it would propose, against the design's goals; one touching no goal goes to the user, as Converging, or not has it, never as a fix. An implementation flaw it settles in the round's change.
- **Weigh the steers —** it weighs the steers the causes press on, as `specs/methodology/working-files.md § Steering Decision § Revisiting a Steer` has it.
- **Propose —** it states the causes and the change it proposes at them, one change for the round, each finding's settlement under it, a finding the change does not settle fixed where it sits, with the reason; where the goals or the frame let findings in, their revision comes first. A round holding a call `§ Design, Refactor, Refine`'s Who decides gives the user, a design flaw or a steer its change presses on among them, goes to the user, who triages its design flaws and answers its other calls, approving revised goals or a revised frame, while the builder settles its implementation flaws and nits as on any round; any other round the builder settles itself.
- **Change and prove —** it revises the design as the round was settled, and before the next review proves the change, as its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

A judgment the builder hands off after the phase's last round has its findings settled together, in one pass, before the next validation review. Where a round completes any run `§ Design, Refactor, Refine` sets, the design is not revised until the builder and the user have reassessed it, as that section has it.
