# Spec of Record: Agent Instructions

This file is the entry point to Spec of Record for these specs. Read it before any work on a file in Spec of Record's scope (`specs/methodology/scope.md § What Spec of Record Governs`). It sends each change to the procedure that governs it, and it holds the rules that reach every author. Read `specs/methodology/glossary.md` next, before anything else (`specs/methodology/scope.md § Progressive Disclosure`). `specs/architecture.md` describes the specs as a whole.

## Writing specs

`specs/methodology/` holds the rules for writing specs and for the code the specs govern. Those rules, and the rules in this file, reach the files in Spec of Record's scope, as `specs/methodology/scope.md § What Spec of Record Governs` sets. The methodology's own files are among them.

**Keeping specs in sync.** What a change reaches beyond the facts it edits is kept in sync with those facts, as `specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Sync` has it. What it reaches is each materialized view of the facts (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Sync § Materialized Views`), and the counterpart that draws their subject from the other perspective (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § Keeping Specs in Sync § Product and Technical Counterparts`).

Every change is one of the kinds that `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § The Kinds of Change` defines: a change to the canon's specs, to the application specs, to code, to the documentation, or to another file. `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document` also sets when a change's design document declares its kind, and how a change that needs two kinds is split. Each change goes to the skill for its work. The skills for the specs serve every kind of spec alike, and each skill forks on the kind where the work differs:

- **Changing a spec, or another file in scope but code.** `.ai/skills/design-specs/`. Its Workflow gives each rule it applies a point-of-use citation, and forks into the reference for the change's kind.
- **Checking a spec, or such a file.** `.ai/skills/audit-specs/`. Its Workflow forks the same way. The skill that writes a plan checks the plan (`specs/methodology/scope.md § Rules and Skills`).
- **Designing and writing code.** `.ai/skills/implement-specs/`.
- **Checking code.** `.ai/skills/verify-spec-implementation/`.

Before doing by hand a kind of work that a registered skill covers, read that skill's `SKILL.md`.

Running the procedure, rather than recalling the rules, puts each rule in mind at the step it governs (`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation.md § One Home Per Fact`). This section's index finds a rule mid-step, whatever the work. The index routes. It stands in for no step of a procedure.

## Design, Refactor, Refine

This section sets how anything here is built or reworked, whether a spec, a rule, a skill, or code. The builder takes the work through this process, in the order that `specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § The Order of a Design's Work` gives. A cold agent then runs an adversarial review over the result. `.ai/skills/design-specs/` applies the process to the canon and the application specs, and `.ai/skills/implement-specs/` applies it to code.

**Read fresh before any work.** Before working on a file, read it again, however small the change or recent the last read. A direct edit is no exception. Nothing tells an agent whether its memory of a file survived compaction. Content written an hour ago feels remembered when it is a reconstruction.

**The design goals, then the frame, come first.** The builder and the user agree in discussion on at least one design goal for the change (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Design Goal`). Each goal is evaluated against the change's problem, so that together the goals answer the problem and none reaches past it. No goal need ask for elegance (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`). Every design owes elegance, as the Design, Refactor, Refine process and Measure twice, cut once have the builder carry it out.

Then, before drafting, the builder states the change's design frame (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`) and presents it to the user. The builder drafts only once the user agrees to the frame or adjusts it. Each answer is recorded as a steer (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Steering Decision`). A steer that changes the goals or the frame is confirmed with the user before it is applied. The builder presents the change, with the frame re-evaluated against any goals that changed, and the user approves it before the work goes on. The design then takes a fresh adversarial review, unless the user's approval has ended its review. Any other steer is recorded as given (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Steering Decision`). The frame's Approach and each of the frame's Outcomes cite the goals they rest on, by their Names (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § Design Goal`), so that a change to a goal finds what to re-evaluate. The frame's Scope, the problem space, cites no goal. A frame the user never saw lets the builder solve a problem the user does not have.

The frame's scope, the cases the change governs, is the actual scope of the change. Too small a scope reaches a local optimum, right for the file in hand and wrong for the system around it. Only the real scope admits the global optimum. Widen the scope until nothing outside it would change the answer. Sweep for it by meaning as well as by the draft's words, because a search built from the draft's terms finds only what the draft already knew about. The frame's outcomes are settled before the draft, so that the draft is judged by the outcomes, rather than the outcomes fitted to the draft. Work the builder would add as the design goes on is held to the goals, as a review's findings are. Work that no goal reaches is a change of its own, however convenient folding it in would be. The builder hands that change to the user to spawn or log, as Design has it, unless the user first widens the goals.

- **Design.** Start from the problem to solve, stated plainly. Get a draft logically working, one that solves the problem correctly, before it is good. A plan describes the draft, and for the specs and for code, the plan is a design document. Where the work turns up a change of its own, that change is spawned as a design of its own or logged as a follow-up, as the user decides (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document`).
- **Refactor.** Make macro improvements to what is being built, to its internal workings and its external surface. These are structural changes, not wording, that make it better meet its actual needs and work toward elegance (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`). Repeat until Refactor converges, when a round leaves the structure where it was.
- **Refine.** Once Refactor has converged, make the elegance of what is built plain, and make it easy to use and to understand. Refine works on wording, naming, phrasing and consistency. Polishing something still moving structurally is wasted effort.

**Measure twice, cut once.** The builder and its reviewers pull against each other, as a software engineer and a quality assurance engineer do. The builder aims to hand a review nothing to find. The reviewer, in the adversarial review and the post-apply audit alike, aims to find what the builder missed. That tension makes the work good. It holds only while the builder does its part, because quality comes from the builder's own passes, and the reviewers are a second set of eyes, never the first check. Fast and sloppy is slow, because each finding a reviewer makes sends the work back through the builder's process and can cost another review. Methodical and correct is fast. So the builder:

- zooms out before it edits, to find how far the rule the edit touches reaches, since reading more costs only time and tokens, while reading too little costs the specs and what is built from them. It zooms out:
  - around: where the section holding the edit has a parent section, that parent whole, the section's siblings among it; where the section has none, being a top-level section, or the edit sits in a file's text before its first section, that section or that text, with the sections on either side of it;
  - up: each ancestor's text before its first child heading, among them a file's text under its title, before its first section heading;
  - across: every passage elsewhere that cites what it read around or any section within it, any ancestor of the section holding the edit, or the file whole, a citation of one part by its key among them. It reads each such passage around and up in turn, and no further;
  - in code or a script: the unit holding the edit, the units it calls and the units that call it;
- writes the reach it finds as one coherent statement of the rule, however many edits that takes, never as a clause wedged into what stood. Patches laid one on another leave a rule in pieces no reader can follow. A reach past the frame's scope is a design flaw, which the builder brings to the user;
- holds each edit to the rules it applies, as it works;
- before any review, traces each mechanism with states or routes that its edits change, such as a State Machine, a DAG or an Algorithm, through every path the mechanism allows, every way into each state of a State Machine among them. It records the trace in its pass. A path left untraced is the next review's finding, and finding it first costs less than the round that would find it;
- checks its own work before any review, as its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

**The adversarial review.** When the builder judges the work optimal, a cold agent reads what the adversarial review's row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` gives it. The reviewer is never the builder, since re-reading your own work re-reads what you meant. The reviewer does not derive a solution of its own. It takes the builder's design through the Design, Refactor, Refine process again, looking for Rube Goldberg machines and Potemkin villages (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`), and for a passage patched where it should have been rewritten whole. The reviewer judges a finding that asks the design to do more, less or other against the design's goals: a mechanism more elaborate than the goals need, or a goal that looks met and is not. It challenges whether the goals' scope reaches the outcomes the design wants. It raises such a finding that touches no goal with its case for why the finding is relevant, since that finding is a sign the goals or their scope may be wrong. It judges an implementation flaw by the frame and the canon. It reports findings, each marked a design flaw, an implementation flaw or a nit (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`), to be settled as Converging, or not has it, and it edits nothing.

The builder briefs the reviewer with more than the task:

- name the failure patterns earlier rounds found;
- ask for any sweep of the repo to be redone by its own method;
- point it at whatever the design decides, since that is where defects concentrate;
- give it the decisions earlier rounds' findings fell on.

The builder checks every finding against the files before acting on it, since a review can be confidently wrong. It settles an adversarial review's findings as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Settling a Round` has it. A round that finds a design flaw, as the user triages it, or an implementation flaw is followed by another round, which checks the fixes. The adversarial review converges at the first round that finds only nits the builder judges harmless, or nothing.

The user's approval of the design's text ends the review too. The user may give it in any state of the work, before any round or during one. The state in hand finishes its own work. A reassessment's work is the user's decision, which the approval gives. A round still running or being settled is left with its findings as they are. The approval is recorded in a pass. The design goes on to its validation and, once stamped, is approved. It is applied where the user approved it with apply, once the apply may begin (`specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § The States It Moves Through`). A change to the design's text after the approval undoes the approval. The design then comes back to the user to approve again, never to another round on its own, unless the user asks for one. After a review that converged instead, a change that moves the design's structure takes another round, and any other change comes back to the user (`specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § The Order of a Design's Work § The Work of a Design`). Then, with the user's go-ahead, the work is applied and audited in the post-apply audit, as that audit's row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

**Converging, or not.** A hard problem can take many review rounds, and each phase counts its own: the adversarial reviews of a design before it is approved, and the audits of its applied work after. A review's finding, whether an adversarial review's or a post-apply audit's, is a design flaw, an implementation flaw or a nit. A flaw is always named a design flaw or an implementation flaw:

- **A design flaw (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`).** Examples include a goal that misses what the problem needs or reaches past it, a case the frame's scope should name and does not, an approach that cannot reach a goal, and an outcome that does not judge one. The builder brings each design flaw to the user after the round, with the builder's recommended change to the design goals, the design frame or both. The user triages it, whoever uncovers it, the one writing the edits or one reviewing them. A finding that touches no goal goes to the user as a question about the goals. It is a design flaw where the user finds the goals wrong, or a change of its own where the user hands it on.
- **An implementation flaw (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`).** An implementation flaw can be of any seriousness. Examples include a case the scope names and the edits miss, a rule broken, and a file left out of sync. Before approval, the builder settles each and proves the fix by its own check, and the reviewer's next round checks it. After the apply, it is settled as `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` has it.
- **A nit (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`).** In an adversarial review, a nit is the builder's call. A round that finds a design flaw or an implementation flaw has all its nits fixed in the change the round makes. In a round that finds only nits, the builder judges each nit. Where the builder judges one harmful, because leaving it would harm the applied files or what is built from them, the builder marks it an implementation flaw. The round then has a flaw, and all its nits are fixed. Where the builder judges none harmful, it leaves them, and records each nit it leaves. A harmless nit is like lint on a moving blanket protecting an item in transit: the lint will not harm the item. Lint on the sterile cloth that holds a surgeon's scalpels is different, because it is not healthy for the patient. After the apply, a nit is settled as Changes After Approval has it.

Every round of the adversarial review that the user's approval did not end is settled at its causes, whatever it finds, as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Settling a Round` has it. The builder counts each run apart, and counts a round's design flaws by the user's triage:

- three adversarial rounds in a row that each found a design flaw mean the design is not converging, its goals or frame wrong past what its rounds can mend;
- five adversarial rounds in a row that each found an implementation flaw mean its edits are not converging;
- three post-apply audits in a row that each sent the design back, for a design flaw or an implementation flaw, mean its apply is not converging.

Any of these runs has the builder stop revising and take the design to the user, and the two step back and reassess. The builder brings its recommendation, with revised goals and frame where the run is of design flaws, and its case for why the work should now converge. They then rescope, split or abandon the design, or take a different approach, and start afresh with what was learned. Or the user has the work go on, and the run is not set back. Each further round that extends the run brings the builder and the user back to reassess, until a round ends the run.

**Each review's model and effort.** The adversarial review and the post-apply audit each run on a model and at a reasoning effort (`specs/methodology/glossary.md`). The user chooses the model and the effort once for a design, as separate choices, before the design's first adversarial review. Where the user's approval ends the review before any round, the user chooses them before the design's post-apply audit instead. The choices are kept for each later review and its post-apply audit until the user changes them. The effort is light, medium or high. By default, a review runs on the model the agent runs on, at the effort the design's Kind gives it (`specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § A Design's Reviews`). A model other than the agent's own can see past a blind spot that a reviewer on the same model would share. A higher effort costs more. Neither changes the review's scope or its brief. A level is applied as `specs/methodology/design-workflow.md § A Review's Effort` has it. A review on a model the agent cannot start a reviewer on runs as the agent allows. The review's pass in the plan names the model and the effort the review ran on, per `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document`.

**Direct edits.** A small tactical edit, narrow in scope and low in risk, may skip this process and be applied directly, but only once the user has approved it. Anything larger or riskier goes through the process.

**A new branch.** The builder proposes a new branch's name and what the branch starts from. The branch is created only once the user approves it. How work is split across branches decides how the work is reviewed and merged, so the split is the user's to settle, as a direct edit is.

**Who decides.** A call the canon gives the user stays the user's, and is settled as its home sets. Such calls include a design flaw, a finding that touches no goal and every finding of a post-apply audit, as Converging, or not and `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` have them. Every other call is the builder's, made on its own recommendation, as Converging, or not has it. An implementation flaw before approval is among the builder's calls. The builder asks a call of the user's that blocks the next step when the call arises. The rest wait and go with it, all together, each with the builder's recommendation, so the user answers in a word.

### Settling a Round

A reviewer's findings are what the reviewer saw of the design. They are often right, and often symptoms of a cause that the reviewer, not being the builder, does not name. A finding fixed where it sits can leave its cause to produce the next round's findings. So the builder settles each round of an adversarial review that the user's approval did not end as design work, never one finding at a time. The builder first checks each finding against the files, as `§ Design, Refactor, Refine` has it. A post-apply audit's findings are settled as `specs/methodology/working-files.md § A Design Document § Changes After Approval` has them. To settle a round, the builder steps back:

- **Group.** The builder groups the round's findings by the decision of the design each touches, such as a rule the design states, a form it chooses, a unit it writes or its frame's reach. The groups take in the findings of the phase's earlier rounds too, and those a judgment the builder handed off has made since the last round. No finding is fixed as it arrives. Of each decision drawing findings, the builder asks what lets them in. The cause may be a design flaw, in the goals or in the frame, as Converging, or not names them. It may be an implementation flaw instead, such as a case the scope names and the edits miss, a rule stated in pieces with no one home, or a form chosen wrong. A decision drawing findings again is the first the builder asks of.
- **Weigh by the goals.** The builder weighs against the design's goals each finding that asks the design to do more, less or other, and each change it would propose. One that touches no goal goes to the user, as Converging, or not has it, never as a fix. The builder settles an implementation flaw in the round's change.
- **Weigh the steers.** The builder weighs the steers that the causes press on, as `specs/methodology/working-files.md § Steering Decision § Revisiting a Steer` has it.
- **Propose.** The builder states the causes and the change it proposes at them, one change for the round, with each finding's settlement under it. A finding the change does not settle is fixed where it sits, with the reason. Where the round's findings, with the phase's earlier ones, cluster on one mechanism, the change rewrites that mechanism whole, rather than adding a fact, a guard or a clause to it as it stands. Each such addition opens a path the next round finds, so a rewrite made now costs less than the rounds a patch would bring. Where the goals or the frame let findings in, their revision comes first. A round goes to the user when it holds a call that `§ Design, Refactor, Refine`'s Who decides gives the user, a design flaw or a steer the round's change presses on among them. The user triages the round's design flaws and answers its other calls, approving revised goals or a revised frame. The builder settles the round's implementation flaws and nits as on any round. Any other round the builder settles itself.
- **Change and prove.** The builder revises the design as the round was settled. Before the next review, or before the design is validated where no review follows, the builder proves the change, as its row of `§ Design, Refactor, Refine § Who Checks What` sets.

When the builder hands off a judgment after the phase's last round, the judgment's findings are settled together, in one pass, before the design is validated. Where a round completes any run that `§ Design, Refactor, Refine` sets, the design is not revised until the builder and the user have reassessed it, as that section has it.
