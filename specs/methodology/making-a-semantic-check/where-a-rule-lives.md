## Where a Rule Lives

This check looks for a rule in the wrong file and for a rule stated in two places. Both need the same pass, so they are one check.

**One pass over every statement of a rule.** The reader makes one pass over every statement of a rule in the files given. The reader reads each statement against those files and against the files that state the rules, [`specs/AGENTS.md`](/specs/AGENTS.md) and `specs/methodology/`.

**List first, then compare.** The reader lists every statement of a rule, in the files given and in the files that state the rules, before judging any of them. Whether a rule already has a home cannot be answered about the first file until the last file has been read. A pass that judges as it goes ends up sampling.

**Within a file and within a section too.** A duplicate inside one section is the one this check misses most often. The two copies are read as one passage in one sitting, and one passage does not seem to disagree with itself. The usual shape is a rule stated once in a table and again in the prose around it. The paragraph restates the table's rule while adding something real, so it reads as a continuation. Counting the places catches it. A rule found in two files is often in three.

**A citation that adds too much, and a citation that is missing.** A passage may cite another file's rule and then add more than [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md § One Home Per Fact`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md#one-home-per-fact) lets a citing place add. Because the citation is there, the addition looks like it follows the rule. The opposite case looks like nothing at all: a passage that builds on another file's rule, or directs work under it, and names no home for the rule. Both are findings.

**Several rules in one sentence.** At a rule's home, the reader reads each sentence stating a rule for one that holds more than one rule, as [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md § One Home Per Fact § One Rule to a Sentence`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md#one-rule-to-a-sentence) has it. A finding names each rule the sentence holds, by the question the rule answers.

**An outside standard.** [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md § External Reference`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md#external-reference) gives a registered standard's version and link one home, its record. So a version or a link stated anywhere else is a second home. A section that adopts a standard, or maps onto one, without citing the standard's record names no home for it. The reader also reads the register, [`specs/methodology/external-references.md § External References`](/specs/methodology/external-references.md#external-references), against that section, for:
- a standard the canon relies on and does not register, or one registered that the section would not register;
- a record whose URL does not reach the version or revision it names, where the reader can fetch the URL;
- a record whose Access disagrees with its URL;
- a paid standard whose meaning the canon relies on without stating it in its own words;
- a form the canon invents where a registered or a published standard fits, against [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md § External Reference § Which Form a Need Takes`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md#which-form-a-need-takes);
- a departure from a standard with no row in its mapping, against [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md § External Reference § A Mapping onto a Standard`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/external-reference.md#a-mapping-onto-a-standard).

**Product and technical.** The reader never compares the two sides with each other for a duplicate. [`specs/methodology/spec-placement/product-or-technical.md § Product or Technical`](/specs/methodology/spec-placement/product-or-technical.md#product-or-technical) gives the reason.

**An overview's restatements.** An `architecture.md` restates its own directory because [`specs/methodology/spec-placement/index-architecture-detail.md § Index, Architecture, Detail`](/specs/methodology/spec-placement/index-architecture-detail.md#index-architecture-detail) tells it to. [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/keeping-specs-in-sync.md § Keeping Specs in Sync § Materialized Views`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/keeping-specs-in-sync.md#materialized-views) says neither an overview nor a diagram is a second home. So those restatements are no findings. A reader who has not read those two sections sees the overview restating half the directory and reports it, at length, as the file that has drifted most. Two restatements are still findings, and are easy to miss while the rest are set aside. One is an `architecture.md` that repeats itself. The other is one that restates something outside both its own tree and what [`specs/methodology/spec-placement/index-architecture-detail.md § What an Overview Describes`](/specs/methodology/spec-placement/index-architecture-detail.md#what-an-overview-describes) names for it.

**The fix.** Where a duplicate is real, the test of [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md § One Home Per Fact`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/one-home-per-fact.md#one-home-per-fact) chooses the home. That section also places a rule several places use. The other copies become citations. [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/which-citations-are-allowed.md § Which Citations Are Allowed`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/which-citations-are-allowed.md#which-citations-are-allowed) forbids some citations, as it does for an application spec restating a methodology rule. There the restatement is cut back to the spec's own content.
