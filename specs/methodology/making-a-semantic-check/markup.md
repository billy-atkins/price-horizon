## Markup

This check reads the parts of how Markdown is written that no script can decide.

**What takes reading.** The judgments left to the reader:
- whether a backtick span holds literal text, as [`specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md § Literal Text`](/specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md#literal-text) has it;
- whether an italic span marks real stress, as [`specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md § Emphasis`](/specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md#emphasis) has it;
- whether a bold lead-in is in sentence case, as [`specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md § Bold Lead-ins`](/specs/methodology/spec-style/markdown.md#bold-lead-ins) has one written.

**The candidates, then the files in full.** Where a candidate list comes with the check, the reader takes it as a guide and then reads the files in full, as [`specs/methodology/making-a-semantic-check/main.md § Making a Semantic Check`](/specs/methodology/making-a-semantic-check/main.md#making-a-semantic-check) has a candidate list used.

**What is easy to miss.** Among bold lead-ins and headings, look for a lead-in that a citation needs to name. [`specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/titling-a-heading.md § Titling a Heading`](/specs/methodology/sourcing-and-citation/titling-a-heading.md#titling-a-heading) has such a paragraph written as a heading.
