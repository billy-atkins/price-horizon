## Glossary Terms

This check reads each use of a glossary term, and each abbreviation.

**The candidates, then the files in full.** Where a candidate list comes with the check, the reader takes it as a guide to where a term seems to be defined a second time. The reader then reads the files in full against the glossary, as [`specs/methodology/making-a-semantic-check/main.md § Making a Semantic Check`](/specs/methodology/making-a-semantic-check/main.md#making-a-semantic-check) has a candidate list used.

**A word used in its ordinary sense.** [`specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md § Glossary`](/specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md#glossary) says when a file may use a glossary word in the word's ordinary sense. Such a use is no finding.

**The fixes.** A second definition is cut, and what its file says about the term stays. A term used in the methodology's sense but with a different meaning is reworded. Where that different meaning keeps coming up, the glossary may be the side that is wrong, and it is corrected.

**An abbreviation.** The reader reads each abbreviation against [`specs/methodology/glossary/using-an-abbreviation.md § Using an Abbreviation`](/specs/methodology/glossary/using-an-abbreviation.md#using-an-abbreviation). Two breaches are easy to miss: an abbreviation inside the house used in a specification or an instruction, and one the methodology or the application coins without recording it. No script can tell that a short form was coined by the house.
