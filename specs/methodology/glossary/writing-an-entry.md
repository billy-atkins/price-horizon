## Writing an Entry

**What earns an entry.** A term earns an entry in [`specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md § Glossary`](/specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md#glossary) when it has a meaning in Spec of Record that training knowledge alone would not give: a term coined by the methodology, overloaded with another meaning nearby, used across areas in a narrow sense, or adopted as shorthand for a principle.

**The term.** An entry gives the bare term, without markup or any of its other names. Each term is listed once, and the glossary's table, [`specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md`](/specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md), is sorted by term.

**The definition.** A definition building on a term the glossary defines opens with that term as its genus, the broader kind the term belongs to, so a term builds on the terms it rests on rather than restating them, and then states what sets the term apart. A standalone definition, building on no term the glossary defines, needs no genus. A use the term must not be mistaken for opens with `Not`.

**A borrowed term.** A term defined in one author's or one body's published work, rather than common to many, ends its definition with that work, before its other names, naming it in prose and never citing it: `After {whose account}, in {work}.`

**Other names.** A term's one abbreviation goes in its Abbreviation column, left empty where it has none. A definition ends with the term's other full names, as `Also called X.`, with several separated by commas. Each name belongs to that term alone.

**Nothing cited.** The glossary's terms cite nothing, so they read as a dictionary: a definition stands on its own and on the terms beside it.
