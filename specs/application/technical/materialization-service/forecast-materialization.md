## Forecast materialization

The Predict computation in `specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation` does not wait for a question to be asked. This is the prepared forecast `specs/application/product/answer-engine/predict.md § Predict — what will the competitor do` describes a question retrieving. It runs nightly, whenever the underlying competitor data refreshes, or on demand when a control plane admin triggers it for their own business unit, across a grid of competitor, brand, geography, and horizon, 1, 3, 6, and 12 months, computing and storing an Effective Price Forecast (`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Effective Price Forecast`) for each combination, the forecast and its rationale together, facts, not a rendered sentence about them, which `specs/application/technical/query-service/layered-output-synthesis.md § Layered Output Synthesis (producing the answer) § Narrative rendering` produces separately. The geography axis of that grid is not independently curated, it is set by `specs/application/technical/materialization-service/reconciliation.md § Product and Geography Reconciliation`, typically country, state or province, and major metro, because that reconciled boundary is the coarsest input in the whole chain and the one that actually determines what can be forecast rather than estimated. Brand A's internal POS data is available far finer, down to individual stores, but that never buys back resolution on the competitor side, and because Position and Simulate both depend on the competitor forecast as an input, the entire answer inherits this same geographic ceiling, not just Predict. A query against anything inside the grid is a lookup against the stored bundle. A query for a horizon or geography outside the grid falls back to the live path in `specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation`, run against the identical model version and data-as-of snapshot as the materialized grid, so a live answer and a materialized one can never disagree due to a timing difference between them. Materializing the full grid eagerly, rather than computing narrower slices on demand, trades infrastructure cost for response time deliberately: the primary user is a time-constrained executive who reacts to a slow or clunky tool by switching to a competing one, not by tolerating it, so a search-box-fast answer across the whole grid a business unit tracks matters more than the compute spent on combinations a given query never touches. Storage and compute are the cheaper side of that trade. A self-service edit to the part of a business unit's own configuration a forecast depends on, the dead-zone threshold, does not retroactively touch a cell already computed under whatever configuration was in effect when it ran; it takes effect starting at that cell's next materialization, nightly, on a data refresh, or on demand. Which configuration a cell was produced under is its own business_unit_configuration_version (`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Effective Price Forecast`). At most one materialization run is in flight per business unit at a time, and at most one more waits behind it: if the nightly schedule, a data refresh, and an on-demand trigger all land close together, the first to start runs to completion, one further trigger queues to run immediately after and picks up whatever data and configuration are current by then, and any trigger beyond that is a no-op, the queued run will reflect it anyway. This is keyed per business unit, not per installation, so one business unit's runs never wait on another's, and it is enforced by the same orchestration engine (`specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`) that runs the job itself, not a separate scheduling layer to keep in sync with it.

`specs/application/technical/architecture.md § Architecture Overview § Diagrams § Layer Composition` and `specs/application/technical/architecture.md § Architecture Overview § Diagrams § The Answer` render this.

A Control plane admin's on-demand run is the one `specs/application/product/tenant-administration/business-unit-settings.md § Business Unit Settings` describes.

## Realized outcome tracking

A forecast is only worth trusting if something eventually checks it against what actually happened, and the data to do that is not missing, it is the same syndicated competitor data that keeps arriving on the cadence in `specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`, it just has not been looked back at yet. This runs on the same cadence as forecast materialization, checking every Effective Price Forecast regardless of its computed_via, a live-triggered row is checked exactly the same way a materialized one is, the mechanism behind `specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md § Tracking Forecast Accuracy`'s own promise that a Control plane admin sees whether a live-triggered forecast turned out to be right, not only a materialized one, by the following Algorithm.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | If no Effective Price Forecast remains whose horizon has elapsed relative to the current date, that has no Realized Outcome yet, and that this run has not yet taken, stop |
| 2 | Take the next such forecast, and check whether the harmonized foundation has actual competitor data covering that same combination and time window |
| 3 | If it does not yet, return to step 1; this cell is checked again on a later run |
| 4 | If it does, decompose the actual data into an effective price the same way the original forecast was |
| 5 | Compute the realized move as that actual effective price less the forecast's starting_effective_price, and forecast_error as the forecast's move less the realized move |
| 6 | Set the realized move's direction by the dead-zone test of `specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Effective price forecasting`'s steps 6 and 7, its size taken as a share of the forecast's starting_effective_price, under the forecast's own dead_zone_threshold |
| 7 | If the realized move lies within the forecast's confidence_interval_lower and confidence_interval_upper, bounds included, set the verdict to Right |
| 8 | Otherwise, if the realized move's direction matches the forecast's, set the verdict to Right direction |
| 9 | Otherwise, set the verdict to Wrong |
| 10 | Store the comparison as a Realized Outcome (`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Realized Outcome`), referencing the original Effective Price Forecast and the Data Vintage the actual figure came from |
| 11 | Return to step 1 |

The original Effective Price Forecast is never edited to reflect what actually happened, it stays exactly what was forecast at the time, the Realized Outcome is a separate, later-arriving record. A verdict is set once, by steps 5 to 9 from what the forecast recorded when it was produced, its starting price, interval, direction and dead-zone threshold, and steps 1 and 2 never take a forecast that already has a Realized Outcome, so no later settings change rewrites it. Accumulated Realized Outcomes are the labeled data the driver attribution weighting rubric and the confidence scoring weighting rubric were declared and versioned rather than learned to eventually calibrate against (`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Confidence and rationale packaging`), and the same data the competitor price-response model's own historical-reaction estimate needs to be trained and validated against, not asserted. How that recalibration works is `§ Open Questions [Name: Recalibration]`.

## Open Questions

**Records:**

- **Name:** Recalibration

  **Open Question:** What trend in Realized Outcomes counts as drift worth acting on, and how are the driver attribution weighting rubric, the confidence scoring weighting rubric, and the competitor price-response model recalibrated against them, once enough Realized Outcomes have accumulated to judge?

  **Provisional Answer:** No trend is defined as drift, and nothing is recalibrated automatically: Acme AI judges the pattern a Platform admin sees across business units, and each rubric and model stays at the version the installation pins until a model release replaces it.

  **Impacts:** what accumulated Realized Outcomes are for, in `§ Realized outcome tracking`; the competitor price-response model's training and validation, in step 1 of the Algorithm in `specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Driver Attribution`; the confidence scoring rubric's reason for being a declared formula, that there is no labeled history of real outcomes yet (`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Confidence and rationale packaging`); a recalibrated model reaching a customer without an application upgrade (`specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`); drift monitoring's watch on the trend in forecast_error (`specs/application/technical/evaluation-and-monitoring.md § Evaluation and monitoring`); and how the pattern a Platform admin sees reaches the platform (`specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md § Tracking Forecast Accuracy`).
