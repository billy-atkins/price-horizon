## Forecast materialization

The Predict computation in [`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#predict-computation) does not wait for a question to be asked. It produces the prepared forecast that [`specs/application/product/answer-engine/predict.md § Predict: what will the competitor do`](/specs/application/product/answer-engine/predict.md#predict-what-will-the-competitor-do) describes a question retrieving. The computation runs nightly, whenever the underlying competitor data refreshes, or on demand when a control plane admin triggers it for their own business unit. Each run covers a grid of competitor, brand, geography, and horizon, with horizons of 1, 3, 6, and 12 months. For each combination, the run computes and stores an Effective Price Forecast ([`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Effective Price Forecast`](/specs/application/technical/data-model.md#effective-price-forecast)): the forecast and its rationale together, as facts. A rendered sentence about those facts is not stored here. [`specs/application/technical/query-service/layered-output-synthesis.md § Layered Output Synthesis (producing the answer) § Narrative rendering`](/specs/application/technical/query-service/layered-output-synthesis.md#narrative-rendering) produces it separately.

The geography axis of that grid is not independently curated. [`specs/application/technical/materialization-service/reconciliation.md § Product and Geography Reconciliation`](/specs/application/technical/materialization-service/reconciliation.md#product-and-geography-reconciliation) sets it, typically to country, state or province, and major metro. That reconciled boundary is the coarsest input in the whole chain, and it determines what can be forecast rather than estimated. Brand A's internal POS data is available far finer, down to individual stores, but that never buys back resolution on the competitor side. Position and Simulate both depend on the competitor forecast as an input, so the entire answer inherits this same geographic ceiling, not only Predict.

A query against anything inside the grid is a lookup against the stored bundle. A query for a horizon or geography outside the grid falls back to the live path in [`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#predict-computation). The live path runs against the identical model version and data-as-of snapshot as the materialized grid. A live answer and a materialized one therefore can never disagree because of a timing difference between them.

Materializing the full grid eagerly, rather than computing narrower slices on demand, trades infrastructure cost for response time by design. The primary user is a time-constrained executive, who reacts to a slow or clunky tool by switching to a competing one, not by tolerating it. For that user, a search-box-fast answer across the whole grid a business unit tracks matters more than the compute spent on combinations a given query never touches. Storage and compute are the cheaper side of that trade.

The dead-zone threshold is the part of a business unit's own configuration that a forecast depends on. A self-service edit to it does not retroactively touch a cell already computed under whatever configuration was in effect when that cell ran. The edit takes effect starting at that cell's next materialization, whether nightly, on a data refresh, or on demand. A cell's business_unit_configuration_version ([`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Effective Price Forecast`](/specs/application/technical/data-model.md#effective-price-forecast)) records which configuration the cell was produced under.

At most one materialization run is in flight per business unit at a time, and at most one more waits behind it. If the nightly schedule, a data refresh, and an on-demand trigger all land close together, the first to start runs to completion. One further trigger queues to run immediately after, and that queued run picks up whatever data and configuration are current by then. Any trigger beyond that is a no-op, because the queued run will reflect it anyway. The limit is keyed per business unit, not per installation, so one business unit's runs never wait on another's. The same orchestration engine ([`specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`](/specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md#engineering-and-production-considerations)) that runs the job enforces the limit. No separate scheduling layer has to be kept in sync with it.

[`specs/application/technical/architecture.md § Architecture Overview § Diagrams § Layer Composition`](/specs/application/technical/architecture.md#layer-composition) and [`specs/application/technical/architecture.md § Architecture Overview § Diagrams § The Answer`](/specs/application/technical/architecture.md#the-answer) render this.

A Control plane admin's on-demand run is the one [`specs/application/product/tenant-administration/business-unit-settings.md § Business Unit Settings`](/specs/application/product/tenant-administration/business-unit-settings.md#business-unit-settings) describes.

## Realized outcome check

A forecast is worth trusting only if something eventually checks it against what happened. The data to check it is not missing. It is the same syndicated competitor data that keeps arriving on the cadence in [`specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`](/specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md#engineering-and-production-considerations). That data has not been looked back at yet. The realized outcome check runs on the same cadence as forecast materialization. It checks every Effective Price Forecast, whatever its computed_via, and checks a live-triggered row the same way as a materialized one. This is the mechanism behind the promise in [`specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md § Tracking Forecast Accuracy`](/specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md#tracking-forecast-accuracy) that a Control plane admin sees whether a live-triggered forecast turned out to be right, not only a materialized one. The realized outcome check is an Algorithm.

**Construct:** `specs/methodology/modeling-constructs.md § Constructs § Algorithm`

**Inputs:** each Effective Price Forecast whose horizon has elapsed and that has no Realized Outcome, and the harmonized foundation's actual competitor data

**Output:** a Realized Outcome for each such forecast whose actual data has arrived

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | If no Effective Price Forecast remains whose horizon has elapsed relative to the current date, that has no Realized Outcome yet, and that this run has not yet taken, End; otherwise, continue |
| 2 | Take the next such forecast |
| 3 | If the harmonized foundation has actual competitor data covering that same combination and time window, continue; otherwise, the forecast being checked again on a later run, Go to step 10 |
| 4 | Decompose the actual data into an effective price the same way the original forecast was |
| 5 | Compute the realized move as that actual effective price less the forecast's starting_effective_price, and forecast_error as the forecast's move less the realized move |
| 6 | Set the realized move's direction by the dead-zone test of [`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Baseline forecast [Step: 6]`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#baseline-forecast), its size taken as a share of the forecast's starting_effective_price, under the forecast's own dead_zone_threshold |
| 7 | If the realized move lies within the forecast's confidence_interval_lower and confidence_interval_upper, bounds included, set the verdict to Right, Go to step 9; otherwise, continue |
| 8 | If the realized move's direction matches the forecast's, set the verdict to Right direction; otherwise, set the verdict to Wrong |
| 9 | Store the comparison as a Realized Outcome ([`specs/application/technical/data-model.md § Core Data Model § Pipeline and Answer Artifacts § Realized Outcome`](/specs/application/technical/data-model.md#realized-outcome)), referencing the original Effective Price Forecast and the Data Vintage the actual figure came from |
| 10 | If a forecast remains that step 1 would take, Go to step 2; otherwise, End |

The original Effective Price Forecast is never edited to reflect what happened. It stays what was forecast at the time. The Realized Outcome is a separate record that arrives later. Steps 5 to 8 set a verdict once, from what the forecast recorded when it was produced: its starting price, interval, direction and dead-zone threshold. Steps 1, 2 and 10 never take a forecast that already has a Realized Outcome, so no later settings change rewrites the verdict. The driver attribution weighting rubric and the confidence scoring weighting rubric were declared and versioned rather than learned ([`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Confidence and rationale package`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#confidence-and-rationale-package)). Accumulated Realized Outcomes are the labeled data those rubrics are to be calibrated against eventually. They are also the data the competitor price-response model's historical-reaction estimate needs to be trained and validated against, not asserted. [`§ Open Questions [Name: Recalibration]`](#open-questions) holds how that recalibration works.

## Open Questions

**Records:**

- **Name:** Recalibration
  **Open Question:** What trend in Realized Outcomes counts as drift worth acting on, and how are the driver attribution weighting rubric, the confidence scoring weighting rubric, and the competitor price-response model recalibrated against them, once enough Realized Outcomes have accumulated to judge?
  **Provisional Answer:** No trend is defined as drift, and nothing is recalibrated automatically: Acme AI judges the pattern a Platform admin sees across business units, and each rubric and model stays at the version the installation pins until a model release replaces it.
  **Impacts:** what accumulated Realized Outcomes are for, in [`§ Realized outcome check`](#realized-outcome-check); the competitor price-response model's training and validation, in [`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Driver Attribution [Step: 1]`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#driver-attribution); the confidence scoring rubric's reason for being a declared formula, that there is no labeled history of real outcomes yet ([`specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md § Predict Computation § Confidence and rationale package`](/specs/application/technical/predict-computation.md#confidence-and-rationale-package)); a recalibrated model reaching a customer without an application upgrade ([`specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md § Engineering and Production Considerations`](/specs/application/technical/engineering-and-production-considerations.md#engineering-and-production-considerations)); drift monitoring's watch on the trend in forecast_error ([`specs/application/technical/evaluation-and-monitoring.md § Evaluation and monitoring`](/specs/application/technical/evaluation-and-monitoring.md#evaluation-and-monitoring)); and how the pattern a Platform admin sees reaches the platform ([`specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md § Tracking Forecast Accuracy`](/specs/application/product/accuracy-governance.md#tracking-forecast-accuracy)).
