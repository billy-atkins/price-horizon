# PriceHorizon, built with Spec of Record

Spec of Record is a spec-driven development methodology for building software with AI agents. The specifications are the durable source of truth. They say what a system does and why, and they stay current through every change. A software engineer designs each change with an AI agent, and the agent writes it. The engineer decides the change's goals and its frame and gives every direction it takes, and approves all of the work the AI agents do: the designs, the plans and the implemented work. Code is built from the specifications and checked against them.

The methodology specifications state the rules of Spec of Record. PriceHorizon, the worked example, is an AI answer engine for competitive pricing, written and governed with Spec of Record: its product specifications state what a user can rely on, and its technical specifications state how the system makes that true. PriceHorizon and Acme AI, the SaaS company behind it, are fictional. The methodology, and the rigor applied to it, are real. Spec of Record and PriceHorizon are both the work of [Billy Atkins](#author).

## At a glance

- Applications drift from their documentation, because nothing links the two explicitly. Most spec-driven methodologies write a single-use specification for each change, so the drift begins with the next change.
- Spec of Record keeps one set of specifications of the system as it stands, and designs every change against it.
- The specifications record why the system behaves as it does, as well as what it does.
- Each fact has one home, and everything relying on it cites that home.
- Structured forms, called constructs, take the place of prose wherever prose would hide a gap.
- Every rule has a check, by a script where a script can decide and by an independent AI reviewer where it takes judgment.
- The methodology evolves through the rules and constructs it defines and the AI skills that apply them, as a self-hosting compiler compiles itself.
- PriceHorizon has no code yet. Generating it from the specifications is the methodology's next phase of evolution.

## Why it matters

Documentation drifts because nothing ties it to the system it describes. Most spec-driven methodologies do not stop that drift. They treat a specification as the prompt for one change: it generates the code, and then it is discarded or filed with the other per-feature documents. The drift begins on the next change. Like a ticket history, the pile of documents records each change. To learn what the system does today, a reader replays every change and hopes none contradicted another.

AI agents raise the stakes. An agent's prose reads as fluent and authoritative even where it is wrong, and it can read as complete while it leaves cases out. An agent that edits one copy of a rule stated in two places leaves the other copy stale, and nothing notices.

Spec of Record works differently. Its specifications describe the system as it stands, and each change is designed against them. Extending a building works the same way. The engineers extend the building's plans and check the new work against what is already there. An issue caught before the concrete is poured costs far less than one found after the foundation has cured. The specifications become the layer of record, and code becomes an implementation detail beneath them, as the JVM is beneath a Java program.

In Spec of Record, why a system behaves as it does is as important as what it does. The code tells an engineer or an agent what. The why often lives only in old Jira tickets, or in the mind of an engineer who has worked on the application for years. Without it, an application is hard to evolve. A change can lose or break important behavior when nobody knows why it is there. Spec of Record makes the why a core part of the methodology. The product and technical specifications state the why beside the what, where the reason arises naturally. Structured Design Decisions, coming to the methodology, will record the choices whose reasons the specifications do not otherwise hold.

## How the methodology holds together

In software design, DRY, short for "Don't Repeat Yourself", keeps each piece of knowledge in one place. Andrew Hunt and David Thomas, in "The Pragmatic Programmer", define it as every piece of knowledge having a single, unambiguous, authoritative representation within a system. A change is then made once, no copy can drift from another, and a reader always finds the authoritative version. Spec of Record applies DRY to specifications. Every rule and definition is stated once, in its home. Everything relying on it cites that home down to the section, with a point-of-use citation placed where the rule is applied. A change then lands in one place, and every place depending on it can be found through its citations. Diagrams and overviews restate their sources the way a materialized view restates its tables. They are kept in sync whenever a source changes, and they are never the source of what they restate.

Where prose would hide a gap, the specifications use constructs. A rule decided by one fact, with at most two outcomes, stays prose. Anything more takes one of an approved set of constructs, drawn from standards an engineer or an agent already knows: the DecisionTable of DMN, the Decision Model and Notation standard, and UML's StateMachine, Activity and Constraint. An entry written again and again, an open question say, is a record of a declared record type, a schema its records conform to, as a JSON object conforms to its JSON Schema. A type is declared once and reused. A field may hold one record or a list of records, of another type or of its own. An employee's direct reports are employees, so the records nest as a tree. Nested records are written as Markdown's nested lists, and each record is checked against its own type. On GitHub, each field stands on a line of its own, as it does in the raw text. Blank lines separate groups that differ, as a code style groups like statements. A reader browsing the specifications sees each field apart and a file's shape at a glance. Every expression is written in FEEL, DMN's expression language. Prose can describe anything, and nothing can check it for what it leaves out. The audit script parses FEEL's whole grammar, so a rule that tests a value nothing defines, or compares a date with a price, is caught before anyone reads it.

Every rule has a check. A script checks whatever programming logic alone can decide, since a script is deterministic and spends no tokens. An independent AI agent makes the checks that require judgment. A rule that leaves no trace in the files, such as reading a file fresh before editing it, is a named step of the skill that applies it, and a record states what skipping that step would leave. A rule's subject decides where it lives. A rule for the specifications lives in the methodology, never in a skill. A rule for the work that more than one skill needs lives above the skills. A rule only one skill needs lives in that skill, which names beside it the check it makes of the rule.

Product capabilities state, as Gherkin scenarios in Given, When, Then form, how their promises would be tested. A promise that could never be checked then shows up as one. A decision the specifications rely on but have not made is recorded as an open question at the lowest altitude covering everything it affects: the file it affects, or the `main.md` of the narrowest folder covering it. The question carries a provisional answer the work moves forward on, so it never blocks the work and is never mistaken for a settled decision.

Spec of Record evolves the way a self-hosting compiler is built, by compiling its own source. Its rules are written in the constructs it defines. Every change to them is designed, reviewed and audited with the same AI skills and tooling that design and author application specifications. So although PriceHorizon has no code yet, the methodology is in real use. Its own rules and PriceHorizon's specifications are both written and governed through it.

Spec of Record is also tested, as software is. Its skills and their scripts are an application written in specifications and Python, and the scripts that hold kept tests are held by them. A script's kept tests stay in the repository and are run again whenever the script, a form of the methodology it reads, or the tests and what they read changes. They hold each action the script offers and each check it makes, twice: once where the check passes, and once where it reports a fault. Each test says which action and which check it holds, and a script reads that from the tests themselves. So an action or a check that loses a test is reported, and what the tests hold cannot quietly shrink. The design script, which validates and logs every design, and the search hold kept tests today. A script that holds none is not yet held. It joins when it gains its first test. The tests run on pytest and write a JUnit XML report, two standards the methodology registers as it registers DMN and UML. What a test reads is a fixture, a short snippet written for the tests that read it. The fixture files are kept apart from everything else, and a search or an audit reads one only when it asks for them, so a sample is never taken for a rule. No test keeps a copy of the methodology's own files. A test that needs one of the methodology's forms reads the file as it stands, so a change to the form reaches the tests.

## How an engineer and AI agents work together

Every change runs the same workflow, except a small edit the engineer approves making directly. Each step exists because skipping it once cost something. The engineer is the human in the loop. Agents author, review and audit, and nothing is approved, applied or committed without the engineer's decision.

Every change starts with its design goals: what the change must achieve, agreed on in discussion between the engineer and the agent. Each goal states an outcome a reviewer can confirm or find unmet, and that outcome is a change from how things stand. Before the frame, cold agents research the areas the change could reach, each recording what it read and how that bears on the design. The agent then proposes the design frame from what they found. The frame sets the scope, the approach and the outcomes the draft will be judged by. It states only what the change changes, and cites the methodology's home for each rule it leaves as it is. Where the design's own words could be read to take in something it leaves out, the design states that as an Out of Scope item, with its reason, and the engineer approves each item as it is written. No step is drafted until the engineer agrees to the goals and the frame. An adjustment the engineer asks for is made, and the frame is shown again for that agreement. The goals and the frame are the engineer's to decide, so a later decision that changes either is confirmed with the engineer before the work goes on. Each decision is recorded in the engineer's own words. A directive the engineer gives binds the design, so a reviewer checks the design against it instead of reopening it. The engineer's agreement to an option the agent framed records how the design came to be, and the agent may revise what it decided, on the record, where that serves the goals better. The work may turn up a problem the design did not cause. The agent brings it to the engineer with a recommended route. A problem that keeps a goal from being delivered is fixed by the design's steps, and one in the text the steps rewrite is taken in by widening the goals. Other work is spawned as a design of its own or logged for later. Decisions given together are one set: the agent asks whether more feedback is coming, weighs the set as a whole, and rebuilds the design once the engineer says no more is coming, bringing the engineer a recommendation to stop or continue each researcher and reviewer still working.

The agent writes the design as a design document: a plan whose steps each say what they change, why, and the Objectives the finished work must meet. An Objective describes an outcome a reviewer can confirm or find unmet, as a goal does. It never describes the chores that reach the outcome or the means that carry it out. It holds no table, names no place in a file and quotes no wording, except wording the engineer chose. It states what its step decides, never a list of the step's edits: within the places a step changes, every decision its Objectives leave unstated stands, and only the text carrying it is rewritten. A step also carries a Draft where it creates or changes one of the methodology's constructs, such as a state machine or a decision table, and where its Objectives alone leave its means in doubt, such as a piece of code. The Draft is the means the agent works out for the step, placed in the plan so the agent's own self-review and the reviewer can read it against the step. The agent weighs a Draft for each step as it drafts it. A Draft binds nothing, and once the apply begins it is history. So the Objectives say what the change achieves, and the words, the names and the structures are written where they land. The agent works the plan through the Design, Refactor, Refine process. It first drafts a solution that works. It then restructures the draft until the structure stops moving, and finally refines the solution for clarity and ease of use. Before stating a step's outcome, the agent has a cold agent research how far the rule it touches reaches. Before each review, it makes its self-review, its own check of the whole work as that review will check it. Before the plan is reviewed, it asks of each state machine or other flow its steps change whether the goals and the frame account for it. Once the work is written, it traces every path through each one. A software engineer does the same, testing their own work before sending it to QA. So the work runs Design, Refactor, Refine, the self-review, the validation, then the adversarial review.

Before each adversarial round, and before the plan's approval, a script validates the design document's structure: its form, its records, the references within it and the line from its research to its steps. It never judges whether the design is right, which is the review's.

A separate AI agent then reviews the plan adversarially. It starts cold, with none of the authoring agent's context, so it shares none of the authoring agent's bias. Every round starts it with the same standard brief, so the design is the one thing that changes between rounds. A reviewer on a different model can also see past blind spots the two would share. The reviewer confirms or denies that the steps bring the goals to fruition and that their Objectives let the finished work be checked, none written so specifically that it drafts the text. It judges whether each step owes a Draft, and reads each Draft against its step's Objectives and the frame. It tests the plan's decisions for their form, as the audit tests the applied text: a decision no check enforces, a record holding more than one kind of thing, a name saying less than what it holds. It flags any part of the plan restating a rule the methodology already states. A directive bounding the design's size bounds what the design takes in, never the form of what it keeps. It looks for what is over-engineered and what is under-engineered, judged against the goals agreed on, and marks each finding a design flaw, an implementation flaw or a nit. The [Spec of Record glossary](/specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md) defines those terms. The authoring agent brings a design flaw to the engineer with its recommended frame change. It recommends changing a goal when the problem shows the goal is wrong, the goal fails its required form, or widening the goals would address a problem on the design’s path.

The authoring agent settles implementation flaws and nits itself, where a goal or a rule requires their fix. For each finding it keeps, the authoring agent names the goal or rule that requires the fix and records who has authority to fix or leave it. When the reviewer drops a finding, the agent can agree to drop it. The report names any applicable requirement and explains why the finding calls for no change. If a proposed fix has no requirement and the reviewer kept the finding, the engineer decides its disposition and the authoring agent records that decision. The authoring agent also records where the finding sits in the sequence of fixes: on the work itself, on an earlier fix, or on the fix of a fix. A script refuses a report that leaves any of these out, or that records a fix made on the agent's authority where the report shows the call is the engineer's. A line reaching the fix of a fix stops for the engineer before it can become scope creep: the engineer widens the goals to take it in, cuts it off, removes it at its root or hands it on as work of its own. Where the agent reads a finding differently from the reviewer, it records why, and its reading never makes a line shallower nor takes a call from the engineer. Each design carries a version in Semantic Versioning's form, which the script works out from its log: in initial development until the engineer agrees to its goals, then 1.0.0. A design reassessment starts a new major version, made afresh, and only that version's reports bind it. The engineer can choose one when review fails to converge. A design whose goals are agreed also takes one when a design it depends on has finished or is gone. Until then, a design that depends on an unfinished design is worked no further than its agreed goals and frame, and waits there. Once what it waited on has finished, the files it was framed against have changed, so the agent frees the design as it next works it and the design is looked at afresh, and its log records what caused each reassessment. So a design that learns its way to new goals is not scope creep.

Each round's report states how the round ended, so whether it binds the design is read from the report itself. Where the round's own settling ended it, the authoring agent looks past the round's findings to what caused them, says why its own check missed each flaw, and revises as a whole the part of the design each cause lies in, rather than patching it. When the engineer's decisions or approval of the plan end a round, the round makes no change of its own, and what the engineer decided carries its findings. A round stopped before it is settled binds nothing. A finding is fixed where it sits, rather than at its cause, only when its fix changes neither the meaning nor the logic, however many places that fix touches.

A round that finds a design flaw is followed by another, which checks the change. A round that finds none goes to the engineer, who approves the plan or asks for another round, since its fixes left the goals and the frame standing. The reviewer judges the plan's structure first, and a round that stops at the structure never converges the review. The review converges at the first round past the structure that finds only nits the authoring agent judges harmless, or nothing. A nit that would harm what is built counts as an implementation flaw.

The workflow's rigor scales with the change. Each design names its kind: a change to the methodology, the application specifications, code, documentation or other files. Its kind sets the reasoning effort its reviews take by default, the least for documentation, whose flaws reach no further. A flaw in the methodology reaches every specification written under it, but only once it is committed, so a methodology change's reviews before the apply judge its decisions at medium effort, and its reviews and audit after the apply, which read its text before the commit, take the most. The engineer chooses the model the reviews run on and can change the effort.

Review cannot spiral unnoticed. A run of three rounds with a design flaw, or of five with an implementation flaw, each round that found something counting however it ended, stops the work unless the engineer's approval of the plan ended the last of them. The agent makes no further fix inside the goals and the frame, and brings the engineer the step back with its recommendation. The engineer can also ask for the step back at any time. There the engineer abandons the design, chooses a design reassessment (a rescope, a split or a different approach), or, where a plan stands, with the goals and frame agreed and the steps drafted, approves the plan as it stands, which sends the design straight to validation. A design reassessment chosen there first reads every flaw the design's checks found, across every version, for the root causes beneath them, and records them in a diagnosis. It reads a flaw a later fix removed as well as one still standing, since a fix can remove a flaw and leave its cause. It then rebuilds the design from its problem, held to the engineer's directives and never to an earlier agreement, and the rebuilt design's self-review shows what in it answers each root cause and each flaw.

Once the review has converged, the script validates the design document once more and stamps it with a content hash, and the engineer approves the plan, approves it and has it applied, or keeps it in progress. Approving the plan authorizes the agent to write within the files the design names, and to create the files the plan's outcomes need under the folders it names, without asking about each edit or naming each new file in the plan. Wherever a plan stands, the engineer can also approve it before the review converges, which ends the review. A later change to the goals, the frame or a step brings the design back to the engineer to approve again.

**Apply and validation.** The agent writes the approved changes with each file open. If a change reaches a methodology script, a form it reads, or its tests and fixtures, the design script runs the kept tests and reads their report. It stamps and logs the agent’s check only when every test passes and every action and check still has its test.

**Post-apply review.** A fresh agent checks the written result against the objectives, frame outcomes and goals. It traces the changed mechanisms, checks the surrounding statements for accuracy, and applies the audit readings to the affected areas. It also checks whether each revision works as a whole and whether its structure and wording hold.

**Corrections and engineer review.** A finding can belong to the writing, the plan, or work the design handed on. The agent corrects writing that falls short of the plan and verifies the correction through self-review. If those passes stall, the engineer chooses whether to keep revising, step back, or accept the last fixes. A flaw in the plan reopens the design with its edits kept for rework. The engineer reviews the applied result and its verification, gives any further feedback for correction, and approves it for audit.

**Audit scope.** A fresh agent uses the same audit skill the engineer can run independently. The default covers every changed area and its downstream areas. Methodology changes reach skills, application specifications, documentation and other files. A skill change reaches the methodology. The engineer can add areas or drop a downstream area. Every changed area stays in scope. This audit reads the agreed files and excludes design documents, review reports and the agent’s follow-up list.

**Audit findings.** The authoring agent compares each finding with the changes, and the engineer decides its disposition. For a pre-existing issue on the design’s path, the agent recommends a route. The engineer can leave other issues alone, assign them to a separate design, or log a follow-up. The agent makes tactical corrections caused by the design in place and verifies them through self-review. A correction that changes the plan reopens the design. These correction passes do not trigger another audit. The engineer alone can initiate an explicit audit bypass, which the agent records in the design log.

**Commit.** Before presenting a commit, the agent checks the log and runs any required audit still outstanding. Once the findings are settled, the engineer can request another audit or authorize a commit containing this design’s changes. If three consecutive applies each end in a send-back, the engineer and agent step back to decide how to proceed. That step back resets the count.

## PriceHorizon, the worked example

Revenue Growth Management teams face a recurring question: what will a competitor do on price, where should our own brand sit in response, and what will happen to the business as a result? PriceHorizon is a proposed answer engine for that question. A user types one plain-language sentence, such as "in six months, where will Competitor Brand's price be in Texas, and what will happen?" The system returns a layered answer. The answer opens with a confidence-scored forecast and a plain-language explanation of what drives it. It maps how the number breaks down by market, and decomposes the forces beneath it. It offers a small set of positioning options instead of one imposed answer, each with its simulated business outcome.

A user can explore a narrower market or switch to a different metric. Below the geography covered by the competitor data, a county or store figure is an estimate when local evidence supports one. If the evidence is insufficient for a local rate, the answer marks it unavailable and shows the regional rate for reference. Local estimates have no driver breakdown.

Every answer is built to meet guarantees that make it trustworthy enough to act on. It is repeatable: the same question, asked again with nothing changed in between, returns the same forecast and figures. Every number traces to a named, versioned model or dataset. Qualitative information, such as regulatory changes and competitive intelligence, is weighed alongside the numbers instead of appended afterward as commentary. The answer also shows how it was built. Each forecast opens to the baseline and the driver corrections it is the sum of, and each confidence figure to its reasons. Where the evidence falls short, the system says so, and it asks about a term in the question it cannot resolve instead of guessing.

PriceHorizon runs as a self-hosted installation inside each customer's own environment, alongside their data. It needs no connection outside that environment unless the customer lets some kinds of model call reach a frontier model provider, and it runs air-gapped where the customer requires. Nothing from one customer's installation is ever combined with another's.

Its product specifications reach across the product, from the answer engine to identity and access, tenant administration, and platform and compliance operations. Its technical specifications reach the data model, the services, the model registry, the stack and the environments.

## Finding your way around

The canon is the part of the project that states and applies the methodology's rules. It holds the methodology specifications, the specifications' entry point, main file and overview, and the skills the methodology registers to apply its rules. With the skills come the code they share and the kept tests of their scripts, the fixture files those tests read among them. The application specifications, PriceHorizon's product and technical specifications, are written under the canon, and the canon is written under its own rules, as a self-hosting compiler compiles itself.

```
AGENTS.md                   the project's instructions for any AI agent
README.md                   this file
specs/
  AGENTS.md                 the entry point, where every change starts
  architecture.md           the overview of the specifications as a whole
  index.md                  the map of the specifications
  methodology/              the rules of Spec of Record
    glossary/
      glossary.md           the terms the methodology gives a meaning of its own
    architecture.md         the overview of the rules
  application/
    product/                PriceHorizon: what a user can rely on
      architecture.md       the product overview, with diagrams
    technical/              PriceHorizon: how it is made true
      architecture.md       the technical overview, with diagrams
.ai/skills/                 the skills that carry the methodology out
tests/spec-of-record/       the kept tests of the skills' scripts
  scripts/                  the tests' code, a folder for each script
  fixtures/                 the fixture files the tests read, and their index
docs/                       user documentation, still to come
```

Every directory under `specs/` holds an `index.md` listing what it holds, and a `main.md` only where it has content to hold. An overview, an `architecture.md`, describes how the files of its directory fit together, and the glossary defines each term the methodology gives a meaning of its own. The `AGENTS.md` files are the agent instructions, what an AI agent reads before it works here.

Each part is written for its reader. The product specifications are written for a product owner, and the technical specifications for a software engineer. The methodology specifications are written for a practitioner: a software engineer designing a project's specifications. The agent instructions and the skills are written for an AI agent arriving with no context but what it reads, and any agent can follow them.

The skills under `.ai/skills/` carry the methodology out. Each is a set of instructions any AI agent can follow, with scripts wherever programming logic alone can decide, and each records the failures that motivated it:
- `design-specs` takes a change to any set of specifications, the methodology's or an application's, through the Design, Refactor, Refine process as a design document, and runs Refactor and Refine cycles over specifications that have already landed.
- `audit-specs` checks the specifications, the agent instructions, the skills and this README against every rule that governs them. Its script clears what programming logic can decide, and a reading audit judges the rest, from an overview that has fallen behind its files to a rule stated twice.
- `implement-specs` designs the code for one product module from its specifications, takes that design through the same reviews as a change to the specifications, and then writes the code, its tests and the wiring between them.
- `verify-spec-implementation` checks that code carries out the specifications it cites. Its script checks the annotations and lists the sections no code cites, and an independent agent judges the rest. It reports what it finds and changes nothing.
- `search-spec-of-record-scope` answers where something is, from an index of the specifications, the skills and the code: every place citing a section, every cited section with the places citing it, the lines a citation names, every use of a term, the code holding a structure, and the places saying the same thing in other words. It answers over the files as they stand, as a commit held them, or over the working files it is given. Each answer is a list of places to read, with a code for the state of the files it answered for, so a later reader can tell whether those files still stand as they did.
- `author-mermaid-diagram` writes and revises the diagrams, keeps each in sync with its sources, and renders each before it ships.
- `configure-spec-of-record` sets up the agent in use for what Spec of Record leaves to it, such as running a review at the reasoning effort the engineer chose.

The product and technical specifications answer to each other. The product layer is the building architect's drawing: everything a person using the building experiences. The technical layer is the structural engineer's: how the building is built and stands safely, inventing no feature the architect did not draw. Each layer names the other, so the two cannot drift apart silently. Diagrams cite their sources, and each source cites the diagram back, so a change to a fact a diagram shows points to the diagram that must follow it.

One fact shows how the pieces fit. The product promises that every answer shows how it was built ([`specs/application/product/trust-and-explainability/guarantees.md`](/specs/application/product/trust-and-explainability/guarantees.md)). A Gherkin scenario beside that promise states how it would be tested. [`specs/application/technical/guarantees-mechanics.md`](/specs/application/technical/guarantees-mechanics.md) gathers the mechanisms that keep the promise. The product overview's From Evidence to Answer diagram draws it, citing that promise as its source.

For the methodology, start with [`specs/AGENTS.md`](/specs/AGENTS.md), then [`specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md`](/specs/methodology/glossary/glossary.md) and the overviews. For PriceHorizon, start with its product and technical overviews. The design documents, the review reports and the list of work still to do are working files, kept out of version control on purpose. What they settle reaches a reader only through the specifications.

## Try it

Some of the methodology only shows in use, so the best test is to take a change through it with your own AI agent. The skills' scripts run on Python 3. The search also needs a few Python packages and a small model kept on your machine, and the first time a script lacks one it stops and prints what is missing, why it is needed and the command that installs it. A Claude Code user can link the skills beforehand, as [Set up AI skills for Claude Code](#set-up-ai-skills-for-claude-code) describes. Have the agent run `configure-spec-of-record` first, so it is set up for what Spec of Record leaves to the agent in use. Then have it read [`specs/AGENTS.md`](/specs/AGENTS.md), which sends each change to the skill that carries it out, and ask it for a change to one PriceHorizon capability, such as a new guarantee an answer makes. The agent starts by agreeing on the change's design goals with you.

You can also run the methodology's own tests. They need Python 3 and pytest, at the release [`specs/methodology/external-references.md § External References [Name: pytest]`](/specs/methodology/external-references.md#external-references) names. The tests of the search need what the search needs too, which the `search-spec-of-record-scope` skill lists under what the search requires. Run them from the project root:

```
python -B -m pytest tests/spec-of-record
```

The `-B` keeps Python from writing compiled files beside the tests, and a run started without it stops and says so. A run writes only within `.ai/tmp/kept-tests/`, a folder kept out of version control, and clears it as it starts and as it ends. What it leaves there is `report.xml`, the run's report in the JUnit XML form: each test run, each test that failed with what it reported, and each test's time. Any tool that reads JUnit XML reads it. [`specs/methodology/skills/authoring-a-skill.md § Authoring a Skill § Kept Tests`](/specs/methodology/skills/authoring-a-skill.md#kept-tests) says how a kept test is written, how to run one test or the tests of one action or one check, and how the design script runs them for a change.

## Where it is going

Spec of Record now governs the code built from its specifications. Its first tools for code are available now: `@app-spec`, an annotation through which code names the sections of the specifications it carries out or checks, and the skills `implement-specs` and `verify-spec-implementation`. The annotation is the link between specifications and code that most spec-driven methodologies lack.

PriceHorizon's code comes next. It will be generated from its specifications once they are rewritten in the constructs as the methodology now defines them, on the stack and environments its technical specifications set. Its Playwright tests will be generated from its Gherkin scenarios. The methodology will also gain structured Design Decisions, and a way to draft an open question's provisional answer and bring it to the engineer.

After that comes reverse engineering specifications from existing code, so a team can bring a codebase it already has under the methodology. `survey-specs` will map an existing codebase into the areas that need specifications and plan how each is inferred. `infer-specs` will read one area's code and reverse engineer its specifications. The thesis is that specifications can be the record a codebase is built from and checked against. It will be tested on [Apache Fineract](https://github.com/apache/fineract/), an open-source core banking platform, starting by reverse engineering its modules into specifications.

## Author

Designed by [Billy Atkins](https://www.linkedin.com/in/billy-atkins) and authored by AI agents, as an applied exercise in spec-driven system design.

I am an engineering leader who has spent over twenty years building software, and the platforms and teams behind it. Most recently I led platform engineering for a cloud core banking platform, where I also helped engineering teams adopt AI. I have engineered framework capabilities that other engineers used to build applications and systems. Spec of Record continues that theme for designing and building applications and systems through the agentic software development life cycle, the agentic SDLC. It is built from what that work taught me.

### Teaching through analogies

I teach through analogies. In my personal and professional life, I bring an audience to a new concept through something they already know, and what is familiar always depends on who is listening. Spec-driven development is not yet an established industry practice, so its ideas need that framing. That is why Spec of Record holds analogies. A building architect draws what people experience, and a structural engineer draws how the building stands. That pair shaped the split between product and technical specifications. Minified code, shorter than its source and unreadable, shows why prose cut too far fails its reader. The Rube Goldberg machine and the Potemkin village name two of the failures the adversarial review looks for: a mechanism far more elaborate than its task, and a front with nothing behind it.

### Lessons from systems I built

Years of Terraform experience taught me the value of a declared definition. A Terraform configuration, written in its own domain-specific language, first describes the infrastructure an engineer wants. Terraform generates a plan, the engineer approves it, and Terraform applies it. The configuration then describes the infrastructure as it now stands. Every change takes the same path: amend the configuration, generate the plan, approve and apply it, and the infrastructure and its configuration are in sync again. Spec of Record works the same way. The engineer designs the change to the specifications, an agent generates an implementation plan, the engineer approves it, and the plan is applied, so the code is in sync with the specifications again. Terraform keeps a state file as its memory of what it built from each part of the configuration. In Spec of Record, the `@app-spec` annotations do that job. They link the code to the specifications that govern it, so an agent never has to infer which specifications govern which code.

I also built frameworks that generated service layers, data access and administration screens from structured metadata. The methodology's constructs carry that idea: metadata describing what should be built. I designed a highly configurable reporting engine that validated that every report definition was free of defects and faithful to what its author meant, before any report ran against it. Spec of Record validates its specifications the same way, by script and by agent review, before anything is built from them.

Spec of Record is not my first spec-driven approach. It is built with the lessons I learned from my previous spec-driven development efforts. In one of them, I discovered the need to nudge an agent at the moment a rule applies. People and agents alike apply an instruction best when it is in front of them as they work. A playbook does this: each step links to the detailed procedure it carries out. That is why Spec of Record places point-of-use citations in its natural workflows, each step citing the single home of the rule that applies there.

### Rigor, review and testing

On a core banking platform processing hundreds of millions of API calls a day for hundreds of financial institutions, a defect has severe consequences. A flaw in the methodology specifications is like a flaw in a mint's engraving plate, reproduced on every banknote it prints: every set of specifications written under them carries it. Rigor still has a point of diminishing returns. Lint on a moving blanket that protects furniture in transit harms nothing, while lint on a sterile cloth holding surgical instruments can harm a patient. A design document is the blanket, discarded once its change lands, so a nit left in it costs nothing. The applied specifications are the sterile cloth. A finding there is settled where it would harm what is built from them. A sentence a fresh reviewer would word differently, though it still has one meaning, is lint that cannot reach the patient.

Every builder has blind spots, me included. Design reviews, code reviews and QA engineers with a breaker mindset caught what I could not see in my own work, so the methodology gives every design an independent adversarial reviewer. In earlier work, I wrote AI skills that audit a shared skills library against its standards. That experience shaped how Spec of Record audits its own specifications and skills.

Testing from the user's perspective came from a gap I found in a previous application. We had many internal tests for parts of our workflows, and none for the workflows our users followed. I led a team that generated Gherkin scenarios and Playwright tests with AI, from the application code, the user documentation and the OpenAPI definitions, and that let us test the application as its users experience it. The user's perspective is one of the most important tests, since the system exists largely for its users' benefit. A restaurant can test every ingredient of a complex dish and find each one good, and the dish can still disappoint the patron who orders it. Testing each ingredient matters, since a bad one can spoil the dish or harm the diner. Testing the finished dish matters too. That is why Spec of Record asks for a Gherkin scenario for every product promise, and why PriceHorizon's code will come with Playwright tests generated from those scenarios.

### How I work

I build each design in passes. I first get it logically working. I then refactor it for structural improvements until it settles, and finally refine it for elegance and ease of use. That became the Design, Refactor, Refine process. In racing, smooth is fast: sloppy work turns out slow, and methodical, correct work turns out fast. That became Measure twice, cut once, in which the authoring agent checks its own work before any reviewer sees it. As an enterprise architect, I learned that no solution can be evaluated until the people building it agree on its goals. So every design in Spec of Record starts from goals the engineer and the agent agree on.

I enjoy solving complex problems with elegant solutions. Spec of Record is one of those problems, and elegance, complexity minimized and never hidden, is the aim of every change to it. I also work as Eric Ries's Lean Startup describes: build, measure, learn, with feedback from real use. At a SaaS company, we ran our own time-keeping product internally and learned more from that than from any client report. Spec of Record was built with itself first. Generating PriceHorizon's code, and then the Fineract test, are the next turns of that loop.
