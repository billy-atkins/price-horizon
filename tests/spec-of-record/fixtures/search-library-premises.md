      ```markdown
      # Donations

      What the library takes as a gift, and what becomes of it.

      ## What Is Accepted

      The library accepts books in clean condition, jigsaws with every piece, and films in their cases.

      The library takes no encyclopaedias, no textbooks older than ten years and no magazines.

      A donor is told at the desk that the library may sell or pass on whatever it cannot shelve. More than two boxes at once are brought by arrangement, so that somebody is there to carry them.

      ## Sorting

      Donations wait in the back room until the Thursday sorting session. A sorter keeps a book for the shelves only where the library has no copy, or where its own copy is worn.

      A book kept for the shelves is given a catalogue record before it is shelved, as `specs/library/catalogue.md § Records` requires.

      Whatever is not kept goes to the book sale, and whatever is too poor to sell goes for paper recycling.

      ## The Book Sale

      A sale is held in the meeting room on the first Saturday of each month. Every book at the sale costs fifty pence, and a child may take one free.

      A book taken off the shelves for good has its catalogue record removed before the day is out.

      The takings are counted by two volunteers and entered in the treasurer's book the same day. What is left after three sales is offered to the hospital trolley and then recycled.
      ```

      ````markdown
      When the library is open, and what is done at the start and the end of a day.

      ## Hours

      The library is open on four days of the week:

      **Reference Key:** Day

      | Day | Opens | Closes |
      |---|---|---|
      | Tuesday | ten in the morning | five in the afternoon |
      | Thursday | two in the afternoon | eight in the evening |
      | Friday | ten in the morning | five in the afternoon |
      | Saturday | ten in the morning | one in the afternoon |

      The hours are painted on the board by the gate, and a change to them is posted there two weeks ahead.

      ## Opening Up

      The first volunteer in unlocks the front door, turns off the alarm and switches on the heating.

      The items left in the flap overnight are checked in before the doors open to members. The float of twenty pounds is counted into the cash tin from the locked drawer.

      A notice stands on the desk through the day:

      ```text
      NOTICE
      Umbrellas drip in the porch stand and are not carried between the shelves.
      Please keep bags off the reading tables.
      ```

      ## Closing Down

      Members are told a quarter of an hour before closing, and the desk takes its last loan five minutes before.

      The cash tin is counted, the takings are written in the day book, and the float goes back in the locked drawer. The last volunteer to leave checks that every window is latched and sets the alarm.

      The heating is turned down and not off between November and March, so that the pipes do not freeze.

      ## Closures

      The library is closed on public holidays, for the stock check in the first week of February, and for the week after Christmas.

      The librarian closes the library in snow or flood, and a notice goes on the gate and on the parish board.

      When the library closes for bad weather, every due date falling on that day moves to the next open day.

      In fine weather the reading-room doors to the garden stand open, and chairs may be carried out.
      ````

      ```markdown
      # Volunteers

      Who staffs the library, and what each volunteer is trusted with.

      ## Rota

      The rota for each month is pinned in the back room by the twentieth of the month before. Two volunteers are on duty whenever the library is open, and three on a Saturday.

      Nobody is put on the rota by themselves until they have shadowed someone for three sessions.

      A volunteer who cannot come finds a swap and writes it on the rota, or telephones the librarian the day before.

      ## Desk Duties

      The volunteer at the desk issues loans, takes returns, takes fines and answers the telephone.

      On Saturday mornings the queue at the desk is served by two volunteers, one for returns and one for loans.

      A volunteer offered old magazines or a set of encyclopaedias thanks the donor and explains that the library cannot use them.

      The desk is never left empty while a member is in the building. A volunteer does not give out one member's address or borrowing record to another person, whoever asks.

      ## Training

      A new volunteer works three shifts beside an experienced one before taking the desk alone.

      Training covers the ledger, the cash tin, the alarm and what to do when a member is taken ill, and follows `specs/library/opening.md` for the start and the end of a day.

      Each volunteer reads the fire notice on the first day and signs that they have read it. A volunteer back after a year away works one shift beside another before taking the desk alone again.

      ## Keys

      Two volunteers hold a key to the front door at any time, and the librarian holds a third. A key is signed for in the key book and is handed back when its holder leaves the rota.

      Whoever locks up makes sure the windows are shut fast and the alarm is armed before going.

      The key to the locked drawer never leaves the building and hangs inside the cupboard in the back room.
      ```

      ```markdown
      # Rooms

      The rooms of the library and what each is used for.

      ## The Rooms

      The library has three rooms open to members, beside the hall they are reached from:

      **Reference Key:** Room

      | Room | Seats | Used For |
      |---|---|---|
      | reading | twelve | quiet reading and study |
      | meeting | thirty | groups, the sale and visiting authors |
      | children | cushions for ten | picture books and story time |

      ## The Reading Room

      The reading room is for quiet reading and study, and conversations are taken to the hall. A desk in the reading room is kept for a reader who leaves a slip on it, until closing that day.

      Newspapers are laid out on the long table each morning, and yesterday's are moved to the rack.

      Food is not eaten in the reading room. Water in a closed bottle is allowed.

      ## The Meeting Room

      Any group from the parish may book the meeting room, free on weekdays and for five pounds on a Saturday. A booking is written in the room diary at the desk, with the name and telephone number of one person who answers for the group.

      A booking not taken up within fifteen minutes of its start is released.

      The group leaves the room as it found it, with the chairs stacked and the windows shut.

      The first Saturday of each month is kept free for the sale, as `specs/library/donations.md § The Book Sale` holds it there.

      ## The Children's Corner

      The children's corner has low shelves, floor cushions and a box of picture books that are not catalogued.

      Children under eight stay with an adult the whole time they are in the library, the children's corner and story time included, since no volunteer can watch them.

      Toys are wiped down at closing every Saturday. A pushchair is left in the porch and not wheeled between the shelves.

      ## Computers

      Two computers stand by the window for members to use, and a member signs the sheet at the desk before sitting down.

      A session at a computer lasts one hour and is extended only when nobody is waiting.

      The rule follows `specs/library/loans.md § Renewals`, which gives way to a waiting member in the same manner.

      Hot drinks are kept away from the computers. Printing costs ten pence a page, paid at the desk.

      A volunteer helps a member to start a session and does not sit with them through it.
      ```

      ```markdown
      ## Story Time

      Story time is held in the children's corner at half past ten on Friday mornings in term time.

      A volunteer reads two or three picture books aloud, the session lasts half an hour, the books read are set out afterwards for borrowing, and each child under eight has a grown-up beside them from start to finish.

      No booking is needed, and a family that is not yet a member may come.

      ## The Reading Group

      The reading group meets in the meeting room on the last Thursday evening of the month. The group chooses its book two months ahead, and the library borrows ten copies from the county store on the card `specs/library/membership.md § Junior Members [Card: group]` describes.

      If a group has not arrived a quarter of an hour after its slot begins, the room is free for anyone.

      Copies are handed back at the next meeting, and the group pays nothing for the weeks between, whatever `specs/library/overdue.md § Fines` would otherwise charge.

      ## Author Visits

      An author is invited twice a year, in spring and autumn, and the visit is advertised on the gate board a month ahead.

      Seats are free, and tickets are given out at the desk so that the room is not overfilled. The author's travel is paid from the book sale's takings, and no fee is offered.

      A volunteer introduces the author, and another sells the author's books at the back of the room.
      ```
